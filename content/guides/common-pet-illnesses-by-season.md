## 1. Spring & Summer: Fleas, Ticks, and Outdoor Allergies

![Dog and cat resting together in a cozy room beside green plants and sunlight near a window](/guides/images/common-pet-illnesses-by-season1.webp)

Warmer temperatures and blooming vegetation bring active outdoor parasites and higher levels of airborne and contact irritants.

### Fleas and Ticks

* **Why they spike:** Mild-to-warm weather speeds up flea and tick life cycles, making wooded trails, tall grass, and shared parks high-risk spots for dogs and indoor-outdoor cats.
* **What to watch for:** Frequent scratching, biting at the base of the tail or flanks, tiny dark flea dirt in the coat, red skin bumps, or ticks latched near the ears, neck, or between toes.
* **Prevention & savings tips:** Keep flea and tick preventives consistent on schedule, check coats after outdoor walks, and wash pet bedding regularly in hot water. ### Seasonal & Environmental Allergies

* **Why they spike:** Pollen, grass, mold spores, and insect bites often trigger allergic flare-ups during spring and summer.
* **What to watch for:** Paw licking or chewing, ear head-shaking or redness, watery eyes, sneezing, or itchy skin on the belly and groin.
* **Care & savings tips:** Wipe paws and coats with a damp cloth after time outside, bathe with vet-recommended soothing shampoos when needed, and consult a veterinarian for targeted allergy relief rather than guessing OTC human remedies. ## 2. Fall: Lingering Pests, Ragweed, and Coat & Skin Shifts

As temperatures cool in autumn, pets still face lingering seasonal triggers while indoor heating begins to dry out the air.

* **Late-season fleas and ticks:** Many pet owners stop parasite prevention too early in fall, even though fleas can survive indoors and ticks stay active until sustained freezing temperatures arrive. Continue your scheduled doses and watch for [seasonal pharmacy deals on PawCoupons](https://pawcoupons.com/) before winter sets in.

* **Dry skin and shedding:** Turning on home heaters lowers indoor humidity, which can leave skin flaky and coats dull—supporting skin barrier health with balanced nutrition and omega fatty acids helps keep pets comfortable. ## 3. Winter: Joint Stiffness, Cold Weather, and Indoor Hazards

![Older dog resting comfortably on a warm orthopedic pet bed near a soft blanket on a cold winter day](/guides/images/common-pet-illnesses-by-season2.webp)

Cold weather and shorter daylight hours put extra strain on aging joints and change how active pets stay indoors.

### Winter Joint Care

* **Why joints ache more in winter:** Cold temperatures and barometric pressure shifts can make stiff joints feel tighter, especially in senior pets, large breeds, or dogs and cats with arthritis or past injuries.
* **Signs of discomfort:** Hesitating on stairs or when jumping onto furniture, stiffness after naps, limping on cold mornings, or reluctance to go out for walks.
* **Care & savings tips:** Provide a warm, supportive orthopedic bed off drafty floors, keep walks shorter and gentler on icy or cold surfaces, maintain a healthy weight so joints carry less load, and ask your vet about joint supplements (such as glucosamine, chondroitin, or omega-3s) or pain management plans.

### Extra Winter Precautions

* **Paws and cold:** Ice melt salts, de-icing chemicals, and cold pavement can dry or crack paw pads—rinse and dry paws after walks and consider paw balm or booties.
* **Indoor safety:** Keep space heaters, candles, and holiday decor out of reach, and make sure pets stay hydrated even when they drink less cold water in winter.
